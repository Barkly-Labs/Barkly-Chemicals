# ChemCraft experience profiles and evidence record

## Scope and safety

The 15 items named after real substances use a locally rendered, fictional presentation layer. Vanilla status effects from the existing items remain separate game mechanics. No profile encodes a dose, preparation, administration, medical outcome, or real pharmacological model. Colors, geometry, timing, and mood are artistic interpretations; sources support only the broad effect categories noted below. Individual experiences vary, and findings for misuse/overdose must not be read as typical effects or as guidance.

The five additional artifacts are explicitly fantasy items; no pharmacological claim or source is assigned to them. Profiles are JSON under `assets/barklychemlab/experience_profiles/`. Each JSON's `rationale` and `reference` are loaded with the corresponding item. A reload listener parses profiles, and a missing or invalid record is skipped with a warning.

## Fantasy-only profiles

`lumen_bloom`, `echo_prism`, `orbit_marble`, `hush_chime`, and `warmth_crystal` have original fantasy rationales and their own colors, geometric motifs and channel strengths. These are not mapped to real substances.

## Modules actually implemented

- **Profile catalog:** JSON-backed profile records contain rationale, source URL, color, pattern, channel weights, and onset/peak/recovery timing. The reload listener builds an immutable map.
- **State and combination:** server-to-own-client packets start a local layer. A repeated profile refreshes rather than duplicates itself. Six simultaneous profile sources maximum; overlapping channels combine with diminishing-return attenuation. Each source progresses through onset, peak, decline, and recovery and is removed afterward. Clearing/disconnecting cleans up the client state.
- **Visual presentation:** Forge GUI and camera events draw per-profile tint, pattern marks, trails, edge ghosting, chromatic offsets and geometry bands. These are GUI/render overlays, not a full-scene post-processing shader; they do not change world textures or actual depth perception. Camera angle feedback exists but is disabled by default. No player movement input or speed is changed.
- **Audio presentation:** optional Forge sound interception attenuates volume, slightly shifts pitch/spatial position, and schedules a quiet delayed duplicate for selected sounds. It excludes music and records and multiplies the original sound volume, so master/category/player volume controls still apply. This is not arbitrary frequency-domain filtering or true stereo DSP.
- **Additives:** four registered, Research Workbench-craftable fictional items have unique textures, language names, tooltips, recipes and an advancement. They apply bounded channel modifiers only while a profile is active, are server-validated, consumed outside creative mode, and have a maximum of four distinct modifiers active. Same additive refreshes rather than duplicates.
- **Accessibility:** the client config provides a master enable, visual/audio/HUD toggles, camera toggle, reduced-motion, trail/chromatic toggles, flash reduction, intensity/transition scaling and F8 immediate reset (rebindable in Controls). Camera feedback and audio are off by default. HUD overlays are limited to corners/edges.

## Deliberate technical limits

Forge 47.4.28 exposes GUI, camera-angle and sound-instance hooks, which are used here. This implementation does **not** install a full-screen world post-chain shader, rewrite depth buffers, blur the rendered world, or alter perceived scale. Arbitrary audio filtering is unavailable through the `SoundInstance` wrapper used here. The visual and audio changes are therefore real and per-profile but are a bounded overlay simulation, not a clinical or sensory-accurate emulator. Optional resource packs can replace profile JSON after reload. Effects are client-local; other players do not see or hear them.
